01 / DOMAIN SCOPE

One policy layer, multiple modes.

The professional work covered travel modes such as flights, hotels, cars, buses, and trains. Integrations involved travel partners including Yatra and Paxes. The flow shown here is illustrative, not an internal production diagram.

01 / BOOKING WORKFLOWS

Different modes, shared principles

Each mode carries its own data and partner requirements. A consistent backend boundary helps keep request validation, policy decisions, and booking orchestration understandable.

02 / POLICY RULES

Keep policy logic explicit

Spring Expression Language (SpEL) can express dynamic policy conditions so rules can be evaluated against request context without scattering every decision through the booking flow.

02 / ENGINEERING CONCERNS

Separate decisions from orchestration.

Rules and partner APIs introduce different kinds of failure. Treating them as explicit boundaries makes a multi-step workflow easier to reason about, test, and support.

01 / POLICY BOUNDARY

Evaluate before committing

Make the policy outcome visible to the booking workflow and keep rule evaluation testable. An explainable rejection is better than an opaque downstream failure.

02 / PARTNER BOUNDARY

Treat vendors as unreliable dependencies

Timeouts, malformed responses, partner outages, and partial failures should be handled explicitly rather than allowed to silently corrupt the booking state.

03 / EVENT BOUNDARY

Use messaging intentionally

Kafka-backed workflows can decouple event handling from synchronous request paths. Event contracts, duplicate delivery, and consumer error handling still need clear design.

04 / OBSERVABILITY

Make decisions diagnosable

Useful correlation identifiers and safe logs help distinguish policy rejection from partner failure without exposing personal travel data or secrets.

Confidentiality note: this page intentionally omits internal endpoints, proprietary policy expressions, partner credentials, production topology, and non-public source code.
03 / TAKEAWAY

Reliable workflows need clear boundaries.

The key engineering challenge is coordinating business rules and external systems while keeping policy decisions explicit and booking outcomes diagnosable. This page summarizes the work at a generalized level rather than presenting private implementation as public code.
